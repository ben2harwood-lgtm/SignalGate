# SignalGate — Growth & Marketing Plan
### Behavioural economics · guerrilla · viral · social — executed honestly

This plan was produced by a designed, validated expert prompt (included at the
bottom). Governing rule inherited from the product audit and non-negotiable:
**sell discipline and auditability, never coverage or returns. No performance
claims until a real record exists. Demo-only, and say so.**

---

## 1. The core insight: trust inversion

The trading-signals category is the most distrusted corner of retail finance.
Every competitor claims wins: photoshopped MT4 screenshots, rented Lamborghinis,
fake equity curves. The audience has been burned so many times that **skepticism
is the default emotional state of every prospect.**

That means the only unclaimed position in the entire category is **verified
honesty** — and it cannot be faked cheaply, which is exactly why it works.

**Positioning line:** *The only trading tool that shows you its losses.*

**Behavioural mechanism — costly signalling:** publishing your losing trades is
expensive. Scammers can't copy it without destroying their own funnel. A signal
that is costly to fake is the only signal a burned audience believes. Everything
in this plan is engineered around producing, publishing, and weaponising that
signal.

**The category villain** (every great campaign has one): the fake-screenshot
signal seller. We never name individuals; we name the *pattern* — and position
SignalGate as the audience's weapon against it.

## 2. Who we're selling to (two-sided wedge)

- **Primary: signal providers** (the Nick archetype). Telegram/Discord providers
  with 50–5,000 followers whose reputation dies every time a follower executes a
  call badly and blames them. Product for them: **an independent, timestamped
  forward-test record of their own signals** — the proof asset no competitor has.
  **Providers are also the distribution:** onboard one provider and his entire
  follower base sees the cards and the ledger. B2B2C — each sale is a channel.
- **Secondary: disciplined signal-followers.** They already pay for signals; they
  lose money on *execution* — late entries, no stop, revenge trades. Product for
  them: rulebook execution + their own audit record.

## 3. The behavioural economics playbook

Every tactic names its lever. If a tactic can't name its mechanism, it's out.

| Lever | Tactic |
|---|---|
| **Loss aversion** | Market the *cost of sloppy execution*, not the joy of winning: "The gap between the signal and your fill is where your money dies." The Discipline Report quantifies missed/late/stopless executions — pain made visible. |
| **Costly signalling** | "We publish our losing trades" as a hero line. Every weekly drop includes the losses. This is the moat — repeat it until it's the brand. |
| **Social proof (real only)** | The live public forward-test ledger IS the social proof. Zero testimonials until real ones exist. Real tester counts only. Fabricating either would destroy the position permanently. |
| **Commitment & consistency** | The YES tap is a commitment device — market it as one: "You approve every trade. Nothing happens without your thumb." The 14-day Demo Desk builds a daily card-response habit; the weekly digest shows streaks. |
| **Endowment effect** | Every tester owns *their* record: "your ledger," exportable, theirs to keep even if they leave. People defend what they own — including a discipline habit. |
| **Honest scarcity** | The pilot is genuinely capped by operational capacity (concierge onboarding is real work). Numbered seats: "Pilot seat 4 of 10." Never fake a counter — the audience checks. |
| **Identity** | Build the tribe: *operators, not gamblers.* "Receipts culture." The user isn't buying a tool; they're joining the disciplined side of a divided category. |
| **Peak–end rule** | Engineer the "Watch It Work" 15-minute session: the **peak** is their own tap making a demo trade + ledger row appear live; the **end** is being handed their first exported record. That's the moment they retell. |
| **Defaults** | The product's defaults ARE the marketing: hard stop always in, 5-minute expiry, demo-only. "Miss a card? It expires. No trade. On purpose." Sell the constraint as the feature it is. |
| **Hyperbolic discounting (countered)** | Free now, compounding value: "Your record gets more valuable every week it exists." The forward test turns time into an asset — the opposite of category urgency tricks. |
| **IKEA effect** | The tester co-produces the record with every tap — which is why they value it above any auto-generated stat. |

## 4. Guerrilla playbook (near-zero budget)

1. **Receipts counter-programming.** Where trading social media posts win-
   screenshots, we post full weekly ledgers — losses included. In a feed of
   green arrows, a red row is a pattern interrupt. Comment sections of
   fake-flex posts are (carefully, non-spammy) our best real estate.
2. **The "Verify Your Provider" challenge.** Open offer to any signal provider:
   *free 30-day independent forward-test of your signals; you get the record.*
   Providers who accept become customers-and-channels. Providers who decline
   send their followers a signal we never have to say out loud. Never
   name-and-shame; the offer does the work.
3. **Anatomy-of-a-scam teardown series.** Educational deconstructions: how fake
   equity curves are made, how MT4 screenshots are photoshopped, how "verified"
   badges are bought. Ranks on search, builds authority, arms our audience —
   and every teardown ends at the honest alternative.
4. **The glass-box livestream.** Periodic live session: a signal arrives →
   card → tap on a real phone → demo execution → ledger row appears. No cuts.
   The product demo *is* the spectacle because nobody else in the category can
   afford to show theirs.
5. **Native habitats, native behaviour.** Prop-firm Discords, trading meetups,
   ForexFactory journals: never pitch — run a public forward-test journal
   (these communities love live journals) and answer questions. The journal is
   the ad.

## 5. Viral mechanics

- **The shareable artifact:** an auto-generated weekly ledger card (clean image:
  week's trades, R multiples, wins AND losses, watermark). It spreads because
  it's counter-positional — nobody else posts losses. Every share plants the
  norm: *demand receipts.*
- **The provider loop (the real engine):** provider joins → followers see cards
  → followers become testers → testers own records → records circulate →
  next provider asks in. Each cycle compounds.
- **"Show me your ledger."** Seed the phrase. If it becomes how traders call
  bluffs, we own the category's language — and we're the only tool that
  produces the answer.
- **Referral = a seat, not cash.** Invites to a genuinely capped pilot. Paying
  people to refer imports the affiliate stink of the scam category; scarcity of
  a real thing doesn't.

## 7. Launch sequence (aligned to the product's 30/60/90 roadmap)

**Phase 0 — Private proof (now → day 30).**
Nick + 3–5 real followers through Watch It Work → Demo Desk. **Start the
forward test — it is the content engine; nothing publishes without it.**
Fix the four false site claims (per audit). Stand up the Telegram channel +
X account. Bank the first 4 weeks of artifacts before going loud.

**Phase 1 — Build in public (day 30 → 60).**
Weekly ledger drops begin everywhere at once (same artifact, every channel).
First glass-box livestream. Launch the Verify Your Provider challenge to
recruit provider #2 and #3. Teardown series starts. Waitlist opens —
positioned as capped, because it is.

**Phase 2 — First proof, first scale (day 60 → 90).**
Publish the first real **Verification Pack** from the ledger — the moment the
whole plan has been building toward; pitch it to skeptic creators as a story
("a trading tool that published its losses for 90 days straight").
Open the next capped Demo Desk cohort from the waitlist. Decide pricing only
now, with counsel (see guardrails).

## 8. Metrics that matter

- Watch It Work sessions run → Demo Desk conversions (the offer's hinge)
- Card-sent → decision rate (product habit health; tests the 5-min expiry)
- Weekly ledger digest open rate (retention)
- Waitlist size + source (which channel actually moves)
- Provider pipeline: challenges offered → accepted → onboarded
- Share/repost rate of weekly ledger cards (viral coefficient proxy)
- Week-2 tester retention (does the habit stick)

## 9. Compliance guardrails (run through everything)

- **No performance, profit, or earnings claims. Ever.** Until a real record
  exists, publish none; after it exists, publish it whole — losses, drawdown,
  account identifier, date range.
- Risk warning on every asset: trading carries risk of loss; most retail
  traders lose money; demo-only v1; execution tooling, not advice.
- No fake urgency, counters, testimonials, badges, or seals — these aren't
  just unethical here, they're *strategically fatal* to a trust-inversion brand.
- One hour of financial-promotions counsel **before** any paid tier, any
  "results" framing, or any live-trading language. (UK FCA financial-promotions
  territory — this is cheap insurance against an expensive mistake.)
- Every published number must trace to a row in the real ledger.

## 10. What we will NOT do (each would import the scam pattern)

- Buy followers or engagement. · Fake scarcity or countdown timers. · Paid
  shoutouts in signal groups (adjacency taint). · Profit-porn creatives, even
  "just once for reach." · Affiliate armies paid per signup. · Testimonials
  before real, consented ones exist. · Any claim the ledger can't back.

The brand is the receipts. One fake anything and the position — the only
unclaimed position in the category — is gone for good.
