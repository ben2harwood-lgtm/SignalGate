# SignalGate — Site Message Map
### What the page must say to *sell* the product — not just prove it's real

The three mockups nailed one job: "can I trust this?" But a page that only says
"we're verified" doesn't tell a buyer **what it is, why they'd want it, or how it
works.** Verification is the *proof layer* — it earns the right to be believed —
but it's one section, not the pitch. This map is the full spine. The chosen
visual direction carries all of it; the verified record becomes "Exhibit," not the
identity.

---

## Who we're selling to, and what they actually want

A retail trader who already **follows signals** (a Telegram channel, a mentor, a
group) and is frustrated that *following* a good call and *executing* it well are
two different things. They miss entries while away from the screen, fill late,
size wrong, move their stop, or let a loser run on emotion. They're also
scam-wary. So the product's real promise isn't "we're verified" — it's:

> **Execute the signals you already follow — instantly, by a fixed rulebook,
> with a hard stop every time — without sitting at the screen, and without handing
> a black box the keys. You approve each trade in one tap.**

Discipline, speed, and control are the product. Verification is how they know it's not a lie.

---

## The page spine (in order)

For each: the buyer's question at that scroll-depth · the job · the SignalGate-specific message · the trust lever.

**1 · Hero — rebalanced**
*Q: "What is this and why should I care?"* · Lead with the mechanism **and the
benefit**, not the audit. Verification drops to one supporting chip among several.
Draft H1: *"The signals you follow — executed by the rulebook, in one tap, with a
hard stop. Even when you're away from the screen."* Supporting anchors: verified
record · hard stop on every trade · you approve each one.
→ *Trust lever: specific mechanism as the value prop (specificity = credibility).*

**2 · The problem (NEW — currently missing)**
*Q: "Do they get my actual pain?"* · Name the gap between following and executing:
missed entries, late fills, wrong size, the moved stop, the emotional override.
Draft: *"Following a good signal is easy. Executing it well — every time, on time,
with the stop in — is the hard part."*
→ *Trust lever: demonstrate you understand the job-to-be-done before you pitch.*

**3 · How it works (NEW — the biggest gap)**
*Q: "What actually happens, concretely?"* · The 4-step pipeline, specific and
demystifying: **(1)** Signal arrives in Telegram → **(2)** parsed into a structured
trade card — *raw text never reaches MetaTrader* → **(3)** you tap **Approve** or
**Ignore** → **(4)** executes in MT5 with a hard stop, staged take-profits
(TP1/TP2/TP3), and every action logged. A diagram of this is worth more than any
adjective.
→ *Trust lever: specificity + "no raw text to MT5" refutes the #1 signal-bot fear.*

**4 · What makes it safe (NEW — decisive for this category)**
*Q: "Will this blow up my account while I sleep?"* · The guardrails, plainly:
hard stop on every trade · **demo-only until you choose otherwise** · you approve
each trade (not blind auto-copy) · it can't fire the same trade twice · expired
signals never trade · pause everything anytime.
→ *Trust lever: risk-reversal at the exact fear; refutational two-sided framing.*

**5 · Why it's different (NEW — anti-scam positioning)**
*Q: "How is this not the usual Telegram get-rich con?"* · The wedge: *not* a black
box, *not* full auto-copy, *not* a profit promise. You stay in the loop; it's
demo-first; it shows losses. Contrast explicitly with the category's playbook.
→ *Trust lever: name the objection in the buyer's words, then separate yourself.*

**6 · The verified record (HAVE — now positioned as proof, not identity)**
*Q: "Does it actually work, and can I check?"* · The equity curve, drawdown,
losing months, read-only third-party reconciliation. Exactly what we built —
just no longer carrying the whole page.
→ *Trust lever: costly, checkable signal; warts-and-all record.*

**7 · Who's behind it (NEW)**
*Q: "Is there a real, accountable human here?"* · Named operator + a real way to
reach them (and regulatory standing stated precisely, or an honest "not authorised,
for X use" line).
→ *Trust lever: named accountability raises the personal cost of lying.*

**8 · The offer / pricing (NEW — you can't sell with no offer)**
*Q: "What do I get, and what does it cost?"* · Plans, what's included, and the
**free demo** as the no-risk entry. The page currently asks for trust but never
makes an ask or states a price.
→ *Trust lever: transparent pricing up front (hidden cost = #1 abandonment driver).*

**9 · Objections / FAQ (NEW)**
*Q: the five things they're privately worried about.* · In their words: "Do I have
to babysit it?" "What broker/platform do I need?" "Can I lose money?" "Is a robot
trading for me?" "What happens when I'm asleep?" Acknowledge → answer → point to proof.
→ *Trust lever: FAQs are a familiar, high-trust container for objection-handling.*

**10 · Final CTA (NEW)**
*Q: "What's the smallest safe first step?"* · *"Try it on a demo — no card, nothing
at risk. One tap to approve, one to ignore."* Risk-removing microcopy under the button.
→ *Trust lever: reversible, low-commitment first step at the decision point.*

**11 · Risk & compliance band (HAVE — keep, make persistent)**
First-class, plain-language, loss-framed — not buried legalese.

## What's missing today vs. what we have

- **Have:** Hero (over-indexed on verification) · Verified record · Risk block.
- **Missing entirely:** the problem · how it works · safety/guardrails · the
  differentiator · the offer/pricing · founder/accountability · FAQ · a real CTA.

That's 8 of 11 sections — which is why it "doesn't sell it." The mockups proved the
*aesthetic and the trust mechanism*; this is the *content* that makes it a product page.
