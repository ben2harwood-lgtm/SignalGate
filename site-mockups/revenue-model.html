Grounded in a real price: £130/month is what Nick's members actually paid, so the model defaults to that — the market clearly bears premium pricing. The catch is the trade-off: the higher the price, the fewer convert. Paid conversion from a free channel is usually 1–5% of the audience, which is why the default sits at 3.5% — push the price up and you'd nudge conversion down. Your share is the other lever: 40% assumes a genuine partnership (you run the platform, execution and record); if a provider only bolts execution onto their own paid tier, expect less. Retail-trader counts vary a lot by source (~10–15M retail FX globally is commonly cited) — treat the market dials as sanity-checks, not facts. Nothing here models costs, payment fees, churn, or the regulatory step-up that comes with executing live money for other people's customers — get proper UK/FCA advice before that stage. Demo-first keeps you clear of it for now.
